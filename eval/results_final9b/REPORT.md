# Final round: deep ingestion (9B summaries) against fast ingestion

Run on 2026-11-02. Every number below comes from files in `eval/results_final9b/` (tables are generated by
`eval/scripts_final9b/build_report.py`; the words around them are written by hand). Protocol, written before any deep result: `eval/FINAL_9B.md`.

> **DRAFT.** HotpotQA deep was still running when this was written; its row says "not run" until it finishes, and the counts will change.

## In short

- **What was compared.** The same documents, the same chunks, the same search code, indexed two ways.
  *Fast*: each chunk is embedded as it is. *Deep*: the application's own summary of the chunk (written by Qwen3.5-9B; its first words name the document title and section)
  is put in front of the chunk and both are embedded together; the summary is also in the keyword index.
- **Search.** 35 comparisons: 1 deep better, 9 deep worse, 25 no clear difference. Deep was clearly better once (keyword-only search on the novel), clearly worse on MultiHop-RAG in all five search settings and
  on the FiQA subset in four of five, and made no clear difference on the other sets.
- **Answers** (Qwen3.5-9B, documents-only prompt, judged by Gemini). No clear difference on docs, thesis, novel and CovidQA; clearly worse on MultiHop-RAG
  (7.2 points lower, 95% interval 3.2 to 11.1).
- **Grounded and cited.** In both arms almost every claim is backed by the documents the model was shown (97 to 100% on all sets except the novel, 90 to 92%),
  citation numbers are valid in 96 to 100% of answers, and no answer cited a document number that was not shown.
- **"I don't know".** When a question cannot be answered, both arms say so 89 to 100% of the time. When it can be answered, the strict prompt makes the model say
  "I don't know" too often on the hard multi-document sets (CovidQA 18 to 19%, MultiHop-RAG 36 to 44%) and rarely on the others (3 to 10%). Deep made it say so clearly more often
  on MultiHop-RAG (44% against 36%; the difference is +7.9 points, interval +3.4 to +12.5) and did not change it clearly on the other sets.
- **So:** with this 9B writer and the application's summary prompt, deep ingestion did not improve the answers anywhere, improved search once (keyword-only search on the novel, a book the 9B knows),
  and made search worse on MultiHop-RAG and on the FiQA subset. It is also much more expensive (see the end). This is one summary model and one run.

## What was run

| | fast (baseline) | deep |
|---|---|---|
| chunks | parent 1,000 to 2,500 tokens, child 150 to 300, overlap 15% | the same chunks |
| text that is embedded | the chunk (with the title as embedding prefix) | the 9B summary, a blank line, then the chunk (same title prefix) |
| embedding model | gemini-embedding-2, 768 dimensions | the same |
| keyword index | the chunk | summary and chunk |
| summary writer | none | Qwen3.5-9B (FP8, vLLM), the application's prompt, at most 180 tokens, temperature 0 |

Five search settings, each returning 100 ranked chunks: dense only; keyword only (BM25); mixed with equal votes; mixed with a keyword vote of 0.02; mixed with a keyword vote of 0.02
plus the bge-reranker-v2-m3 reranker (all fused candidates). Metrics: nDCG@10, Hit@5, Recall@100. Differences are deep minus fast on the same questions, with a paired bootstrap
(10,000 resamples); "better" or "worse" is written only when the 95% interval excludes zero.

Answers: the mixed search with keyword vote 0.02 plus the reranker, six parent chunks (at most 12,000 characters), Qwen3.5-9B at temperature 0, prompt `strict-v1`
(answer only from the numbered documents, one fixed sentence when they do not say, `[n]` after each statement). The summaries are not shown to the answer model.
Judge: Gemini 3.7 flash (a different model from the answerer) for correctness against a reference answer (correct 1, partly 0.5, wrong or refused 0; for a question that cannot
be answered, refusing scores 1) and for grounding (are the answer's claims stated in the shown documents; does the cited document back the claim).
Code: branch `eval/rebase-on-main` on `main` (commit b720085) with two uncommitted search patches (BM25 fix, keyword-vote setting); nothing is committed.

## Why deep did not help (analysis made after the results; `NOTES_why_deep.md`)

- **No bug.** On 600 random chunks per set (FiQA subset, SciFact, MultiHop-RAG, CovidQA) every summary belongs to its own chunk, and every stored vector, fast and deep, is exactly the cached vector of the
  text it should be (cosine 1.00000). No chunk lacks a summary.
- **The summary adds little on these sets.** Their documents are short (an abstract, a forum post, a paragraph), so a chunk is most or all of its document and there is nothing for a summary to "situate".
  The summary is a paraphrase; embedding it with the chunk moves every vector (cosine to the fast vector of the same chunk 0.90 to 0.97) and lowers every question-to-document similarity by 0.02 to 0.04.
- **That is harmless when competing documents fall by the same amount** (SciFact, CovidQA) **and harmful when the summary drifts from the text.** On FiQA, 53% of the summary's content words occur nowhere in the
  text the 9B was shown (22 to 34% on the other sets), every summary opens with a bare document number ("In 63 (Text Document), ..." because the posts have no title), random chunks become more alike
  (cosine 0.577 to 0.616) and the gap between a question and its relevant post shrinks by 11% (0.253 to 0.224). That fits the 0.068 nDCG@10 loss.
- **MultiHop-RAG is only partly explained.** The gap to random chunks hardly changes (0.230 to 0.224), but relevant articles lose a little more similarity than competing articles
  (-0.0132 against -0.0105), enough to push them down a few places among near-tied candidates (the best relevant article moves down for 21% of the questions and up for 6%).
  The repeated title and "hub" documents were tested and are not the cause.
- **The one clear gain** (keyword-only search on the novel) is most likely the 9B putting character names into the text from its memory of the book (70% of its novel summaries name something it was not shown).
  That is not a general gain.
- **Not proven.** A controlled test would embed the chunk with only the summary's opening words, and the summary body without them, on a sample of chunks (about 3,000 embeddings, roughly $0.3 to $0.5). It was not run.

## Things to know before using these numbers

- **FiQA is a subset.** FiQA was stopped at about 45,000 of 71,000 summaries (to save time), which cover the first 36,000 documents. Both arms are compared on those documents plus every relevant document
  ("fiqa-sub", all 648 questions). It is not the standard FiQA benchmark and must not be compared with published FiQA numbers. The whole-corpus fast numbers are above.
- **CovidQA mixed search is held back by the product's near-duplicate filter** in both arms: it keeps one chunk per first 120 characters, 28% of the documents have such a twin,
  and Recall@100 falls from 1.000 (dense only) to 0.875 (mixed) in the fast arm and from 0.992 to 0.874 in the deep arm. The filter was not changed (`NOTES_prefix_twins_fast.md`, `NOTES_prefix_twins_deep.md`).
- **Small counts.** The sets with reference answers are small (31 to 35 answerable questions in docs, thesis and novel); abstention on unanswerable questions is 5 to 9 questions per set
  (35 on MultiHop-RAG). Read those as counts. The novel is the held-out test; the 9B knows the book.
- **One summary model, one run, temperature 0, no human labels.** The CovidQA reference answers were written by a model. The judge is Gemini and the embedding model is Gemini; the answerer and the summary writer are Qwen.
- **The old fast report was not reused.** The fast arm was run again on the same code, chunker, answer model and prompt as the deep arm. Dense-only and keyword-only search match the old report within 0.02 nDCG@10.
- **Not part of this round:** another answer model, another summary model, human labels, RGB, latency.
